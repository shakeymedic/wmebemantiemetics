A Clinician's Guide to Antiemetics in the ED

Navigating Nausea and Vomiting with an Evidence-Based Approach

Understanding the Vomiting Pathways

Nausea and vomiting are protective reflexes coordinated by the vomiting centre in the brainstem. This centre receives signals from four main pathways. Effective treatment involves selecting a drug that targets the most likely pathway responsible for the patient's symptoms.

🧠

Higher Brain Centres

Triggered by stress, anxiety, or anticipation. Involves GABA and H1 receptors.

👂

Vestibular System

Detects motion and balance changes. Key for vertigo and motion sickness. Involves H1 and muscarinic receptors.

🧪

Chemoreceptor Trigger Zone (CTZ)

Senses toxins, drugs, and metabolic changes in the blood. Rich in D2 and 5-HT3 receptors.

🍋

Gastrointestinal Tract

Vagal nerve afferents respond to irritation or distension. Rich in 5-HT3 receptors.

Our Toolkit: How Common ED Antiemetics Work

Each class of antiemetic targets specific neurotransmitter receptors. Matching the drug's mechanism to the patient's clinical picture is key to successful treatment.

Dopamine (D₂) Antagonists

Agents: Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine, Haloperidol, Droperidol

Primarily block D₂ receptors in the CTZ, making them effective for nausea from metabolic causes, toxins, and migraines.

Serotonin (5-HT₃) Antagonists

Agents: Ondansetron

Block 5-HT₃ receptors in the CTZ and on vagal nerve endings in the GI tract. Excellent for gastroenteritis and general undifferentiated nausea.

Antihistamines (H₁)

Agents: Cyclizine, Promethazine, Dimenhydrinate

Block H₁ receptors in the vestibular system and vomiting centre. The first-line choice for vertigo. Some, like promethazine, also have weak D₂ antagonist effects.

Safety First: Key Adverse Effects

Effectiveness must be balanced with the potential for adverse effects. Consider these when selecting an agent.

🏃‍♂️

Akathisia & Dystonia

Distressing restlessness or muscle spasms. A key risk for D₂ antagonists.

High Risk: Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine

❤️

QTc Prolongation

Can increase arrhythmia risk. Dose-dependent concern for several agents.

High Risk: Droperidol, Haloperidol, Ondansetron (high doses)

😴

Sedation

Can be significant and may impair safe discharge. A hallmark of antihistamines.

High Risk: Cyclizine, Promethazine, Dimenhydrinate
